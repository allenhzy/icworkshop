The Kirin 9050 Pro is the first 3D IC built using Huawei’s Logic Folding technology, featuring fine-grained logic-on-logic stacking with a 1.5-um hybrid-bonding pitch and 50 million vertical connections. Commercialized in Huawei’s recent tri-fold smartphone, it marks an important step toward bringing fine-grained 3D ICs to mobile computing, potentially paralleling the transformative role of HBM in AI chips. Emerging technical reports and teardown analyses offer glimpses into this 7-nm 3D IC competing with 2-nm 2D ICs. With commercial EDA tools lacking native support for Logic Folding, Huawei has identified EDA as a grand challenge in enabling such a complex 3D design. Based entirely on public-domain information, this talk provides an EDA perspective on the Kirin 9050 Pro, deducing how EDA technologies may have been developed and optimized for PPA while addressing thermal, power-delivery, and other multi-physics reliability challenges. We conclude by identifying limitations of today’s EDA methodologies and directions to further unlock the potential of fine-grained 3D ICs for mobile applications.

Continued scaling of compute performance demands ever more on-die memory and increased compute density. Yet traditional area scaling is slowing and can no longer keep pace with these demands on its own. Chiplets and 3D stacking, enabled by hybrid bonding, offer a path forward, allowing products to efficiently grow cache size and compute density beyond what monolithic scaling can deliver.

This talk explores how AMD has applied 3D stacking across successive product generations. We begin with the AMD 3D V-Cache™, covering the underlying hybrid bonding technology, key design choices, and the architecture that enabled stacked cache integration. We then discuss the design-technology co-optimization (DTCO) improvements introduced in the second-generation V-Cache, highlighting how technology and design can evolve together to further improve performance, power, and area efficiency. Finally, we turn to the AMD Instinct™ MI300X accelerator, which takes 3D architecture and technology co-design to the next level. We describe how hybrid-bonded chiplets are integrated to form a cohesive design, illustrating how advanced 3D packaging provides the flexibility and scalability needed to drive future compute products.

In this talk, we will explore a possible path towards the next generation of 3D system integration: the CMOS2.0 paradigm. Built around the vision of a multilayer, highly specialized and heterogeneous IC stack, CMOS2.0 opens new opportunities for breaking down complex systems into finer-grained functional building blocks, each implemented in a technology best suited to its specific role. We will explore how this unprecedented level of system partitioning and technology specialization could reshape the way we design integrated systems. We will discuss the architectural changes required—not only at the hardware level, but potentially extending all the way to application design—to fully unlock the potential of this technology. This calls for a holistic approach to System-Technology Co-Optimization (STCO), in which technology, architecture and applications are developed in close interaction rather than as separate design steps. Finally, we will examine the new challenges and opportunities that the CMOS2.0 paradigm brings to Electronic Design Automation (EDA) and consider how design methodologies and tools will need to evolve to support this new way of building systems.

Pseudo-3D design flows have enabled 3D ICs to be implemented with commercial-level quality by leveraging mature 2D engines. As these engines are inherently 2D-native, a gap remains in optimizations specific to 3D integration. Recent efforts have narrowed this gap through dedicated heuristics and AI/ML-based prediction and optimization, gradually reducing the dependence on the underlying tool engines. This trend points toward a 3D-native design methodology and EDA framework as the eventual destination. This talk reviews this progression from pseudo-3D toward 3D-native design, and outlines the future directions from a 'design' perspective: developing 3D-native algorithms that reflect the distinct characteristics of the 3D domain, and employing AI/ML and differentiable optimization to bring their design quality close to the commercial level.
